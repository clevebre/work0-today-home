Help now

If you are in danger now, call 999. Speak to the operator if you can, even in a whisper; you may be asked to cough or tap the keys to answer questions. If you cannot speak, listen carefully to the call handler's questions and instructions. On a mobile, if making a sound would put you or someone else in danger, you will be asked to press 55 to be put through to the police: if you do not press it, the call ends. Pressing 55 does not work from a landline; on a landline, stay on the line: if the operator hears only background noise, your call is put through to the police.

Drugs and alcohol, in Northern Ireland

A doctor can help you get advice and a referral to services for drug or alcohol problems, and some services accept people who refer themselves. To find one near you: the Northern Ireland drugs and alcohol services directory. If someone has taken drugs or drunk alcohol and is very unwell, call 999 and ask for an ambulance.

If someone else's drinking or drug use is causing you problems, there is support for you too, even if they are not getting help themselves: help if you are affected by someone else's drinking or drug use, from the Public Health Agency.
